Whose land will you stand on in Denver? The AAR/SBL Annual Meeting gathers this year near the confluence of Cherry Creek and the South Platte River, the homelands of the Cheyenne, Arapaho, and Ute nations, and the NABPR acknowledges with respect the peoples who carry those relationships to this place today. I ask on purpose: one of our two sessions asks how religious knowledge travels from the seminar room into public life, and any honest answer begins with the ground beneath the hotel ballroom.

NABPR invites you to two sponsored sessions. Read together, I will argue (briefly, and with affection), they make the case for the Baptist professor of religion as a public figure writ large: on Saturday we gather around the lectern; on Monday we carry the lectern into your earbuds. (Same room both days, so you only have to find it once.)

**Where:** Hyatt Regency Denver, 3rd Floor, Mineral A

## Saturday, November 21, 3:00–4:30: Presidential & Plenary Addresses

The Annual November Meeting of the National Association of Baptist Professors of Religion welcomes all AAR/SBL attendees, members and the merely curious alike. **Laura Levens** (Baptist Seminary of Kentucky) delivers the presidential address. **Oluwatomisin Olayinka Oredein** (Brite Divinity School) delivers the plenary address; Oredein is the author of *The Theology of Mercy Amba Oduyoye*, which follows the life of Mercy Oduyoye and the formation of African women's theology. Oduyoye's work insists that theology lives in bodies, households, and communities; I expect this plenary to press that claim on all of us.

## Monday, November 23, 3:00–4:30: Producing Public Religious Education in our Current Time

The podcast fills a lacuna that the journal article and the Sunday sermon both leave open: a space where careful scholarship meets a listener on a commute, at the sink, or on a trail. This session convenes scholars, practitioners, and media producers working at the intersection of religion, public life, and digital humanities to explore how we create, translate, and share religious knowledge in contemporary public contexts. We want to talk shop about the craft (and the stakes) of teaching religion on air. Four podcasts constellate the panel:

### 1. "Healing Our Sacred Lake"

<img src="/images/podcast-healing-our-sacred-lake.webp" alt="Healing the Sacred podcast art: fish, an eel, and debris on the floor of Onondaga Lake, with the subtitle Restoring Onondaga Lake through Story, Ceremony, and Public Scholarship" width="220" loading="lazy">

This show follows the Haudenosaunee restoration of Onondaga Lake, NY, the sacred site where the Peacemaker planted the Great Law of Peace and, centuries later, one of the most polluted lakes in "America." **Betty Hill Lyons** (American Indian Law Alliance), **Rebekka King** (St. Francis Xavier University), and **Adam DJ Brett** bring story, ceremony, and public scholarship to bear on what healing a sacred place requires.

### 2. "Bible Divas"

<img src="/images/podcast-bible-divas.webp" alt="Bible Divas podcast art from Baptist News Global, with a neon logo and photos of the hosts" width="220" loading="lazy">

Biblical scholarship meets honest public conversation, from Baptist News Global. **Angela N. Parker**, **Anna Sieges Beal**, and **Cynthia Shafer-Elliott** make the case that the guild's best work belongs in public (and that it can be fun).

### 3. "Voice of the Turtle"

<img src="/images/podcast-voice-of-the-turtle.webp" alt="Voice of the Turtle podcast art: the official AWAB podcast, with a turtle between the words" width="220" loading="lazy">

The official podcast of the Association of Welcoming and Affirming Baptists chronicles the welcoming and affirming movement in Baptist life. **Brian Henderson** and **Mitch Randall** join the panel.

### 4. "Spirit & Power"

<img src="/images/podcast-spirit-and-power.webp" alt="Spirit and Power podcast art: Charismatics and Politics in American Life with Dr. Leah Payne" width="220" loading="lazy">

Politics meets Pentecostal Christians here, or, as the show puts it, charismatics and politics in American life. **Leah Payne** tracks the Spirit-filled movements reshaping the public square.

## See You in Mineral A

Bring your questions, your students, and your favorite episode. Find us at the Hyatt Regency Denver, 3rd Floor, Mineral A; Saturday and Monday, 3:00–4:30.
